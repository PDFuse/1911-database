M1911 & M1911A1 Barrel Reference Guide

Barrels are one of the most important parts to review when evaluating U.S. military M1911 and M1911A1 pistols. Barrel markings, finish, wear, and proof marks should be compared with the pistol serial range, manufacturer, and service history.

Common Barrel Markings

MarkingCommon IdentificationTypical Location

P		 Proof Mark Left barrel lug or chamber hood depending on maker and period

HS		 High Standard Right barrel lug

F		 Flannery Bolt Company Right barrel lug

C-in-Square		 Colt Replacement Barrel Right barrel lug

COLT 45 AUTO		 Colt Caliber Marking Left side of chamber

WWII Barrel Identification Guide

Marking Manufacturer Typical Use
HS High Standard Common on WWII Ithaca, Remington Rand, and Union Switch & Signal pistols.
F Flannery Bolt Company Found on some late-war production and replacement barrels.
COLT 45 AUTO Colt Used on Colt military and commercial barrels.
HP Colt Common on earlier Colt barrels, especially WWI and transitional production.
S Springfield Armory Associated with Springfield Armory production barrels.
C-in-Square Colt Commonly encountered on post-war Colt replacement barrels.
7791193 Government Contract Post-war replacement barrel drawing number.

Barrel Review Checklist

AreaWhat To Review
MarkingsLook for manufacturer, proof, caliber, and inspection marks.
FinishCompare barrel finish to the frame, slide, and expected production period.
WearWear should be compared to the rest of the pistol.
Period FitCheck whether the barrel style fits the pistol serial range and production period.
Service ReplacementReplacement barrels are common on military pistols and should be documented accurately.

Common Barrel Categories

CategoryCollector Notes
Original Production Barrel Best match when markings, finish, wear, and period features align with the pistol.
Period Replacement Barrel May still be historically appropriate if installed during service life.
Arsenal Replacement Barrel Often seen on rebuilt pistols and should be considered part of the pistol's service history.
Modern Replacement Barrel Functional but not original military production.
Collector Note: A barrel should never be evaluated by marking alone. Compare serial range, finish, wear pattern, proof marks, small parts, and service history together.
