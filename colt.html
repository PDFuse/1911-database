Colt M1911 & M1911A1 Serial Number Ranges

Colt was the original manufacturer of the U.S. military M1911 and a major producer of the M1911A1. This page provides Colt serial number ranges, documented examples, and collector guidance for identifying, researching, and documenting Colt military pistols.

WWI Colt M1911

Early Colt pistols should be evaluated by serial range, slide legend, frame markings, finish type, and period inspector initials.

1924 Transitional Colts

The 1924 transition pistols are a major collector subset because they bridge the original Model 1911 and later M1911A1 configuration.

WWII Colt M1911A1

WWII Colt pistols are commonly evaluated by inspection marks, parkerized finish, slide markings, barrel markings, and small parts.

Ithaca Duplicate Range

Serial numbers 856405 through 916404 require special review because Ithaca duplicated this range.

1924 Colt Transitional Model Notes

Research Note: The 1924 Colt transition pistols are not ordinary WWI pistols and should not be lumped into later WWII M1911A1 production. They occupy an important collector space between the original Model 1911 and the standardized M1911A1.
Area What To Check
Serial Context Transition pistols are generally associated with the 700000 serial range and the 1924 production group delivered to Springfield Armory. Serial number should be treated as the starting point only.
Slide Marking These pistols may still carry the earlier MODEL OF 1911 U.S. ARMY slide marking even though they incorporate features leading toward the M1911A1 pattern.
Internal Slide Marks Review the rear of the slide and underside for correct H, G, or other expected markings. Some transition examples are described with correctly unnumbered slides.
Inspection Look for correct transition-period inspection marks such as W.T.G. or other marks appropriate to the exact serial range. Clear photographs are important because faint marks can drive authentication and value.
Barrel Photograph HP, intertwined HP, K, or other barrel marks. Barrel originality has a major effect on value.
Finish Transition pistols may retain high-polish or brushed blue finish characteristics. Check for sharp markings, correct edge wear, and whether any markings appear buffed or softened.
Magazine Two-tone and pinned-base magazines should be documented from the side, base, and spine when present.

WWII Colt M1911A1 Identification Notes

Feature Collector Guidance
G.H.D. Inspection G.H.D. is commonly associated with WWII Colt M1911A1 production. Photograph the mark clearly and compare it with serial range, finish, and other frame markings.
J.S.B. Inspection J.S.B. appears on some late-war Colt examples and should be checked against the exact serial number, slide markings, and ordnance acceptance mark.
Slide Roll Marks WWII Colt slides should be checked for patent block, Rampant Colt placement, Hartford address, and proof location. Replacement slides are common on service pistols.
P Proofs Check for top-of-slide and frame P proof marks. Misalignment, missing proofs, or inconsistent finish around proofs should be photographed and explained.
Ordnance Mark The crossed-cannon ordnance mark should be reviewed for sharpness, location, and consistency with finish wear.
Barrel Marks Colt barrels may show Colt .45 Auto, P, G, F, or other markings depending on period and part. Barrel replacement is common in U.S. service pistols.
Grips and Small Parts Review grip type, hammer, trigger, mainspring housing, thumb safety, slide stop, grip safety, and magazine catch against the serial range and service history.

Colt Rebuild and Service-History Notes

Colt pistols often remained in U.S. service for decades. Arsenal rebuilds, replacement barrels, replacement slides, and mixed small parts should be described accurately rather than dismissed as fake. A rebuild can be an authentic U.S. military pistol, but it is different from an untouched factory-original pistol.

Mark / Evidence Meaning for Research
OG Ogden Arsenal rebuild or inspection marking. A Colt with OG should be treated as an arsenal-history pistol, not an untouched factory-original example.
RIA / SA / AA / ANAD / LEAD These and similar depot marks indicate official rebuild, inspection, or service processing. They can support military provenance while reducing factory-original collector grade.
Replacement Barrel A correct service replacement barrel may be historically appropriate, especially on a rebuild, but should be documented separately from factory-original claims.
Finish Difference Compare finish color and texture between slide, frame, barrel, and small parts. Uneven wear can indicate part replacement; overly even finish can indicate refinish.

Colt Photo Documentation Checklist

Frame Markings

Serial number, United States Property, M1911A1 U.S. Army, inspector mark, P proof, and ordnance mark.

Slide Markings

Left and right side roll marks, top P proof, rear of slide, underside marks, and firing pin stop area.

Barrel

Top, chamber, left lug, right lug, bottom, bore condition, and proof marks.

Small Parts

Hammer, trigger, thumb safety, grip safety, slide stop, magazine catch, mainspring housing, and lanyard loop.

Grips

Front and backside of both panels, mold numbers, reinforcing rings, screw escutcheons, cracks, and wear.

Accessories

Magazine markings, holster maker/date such as BOYT 42, CMP paperwork, sales tags, and auction records.

Colt Collector Overview

Area What To Review
Serial Number Use the serial number to determine the likely production year and model period. The serial number is only the starting point and should not be used alone to authenticate a pistol.
Frame Markings Review United States Property marking, model marking, serial number font, inspector initials, proof marks, and ordnance acceptance stamp.
Slide Markings Compare the slide legend, patent/address lines, logo placement, and overall style to the serial range and production period.
Finish Early pistols may show original blue finishes, while later wartime pistols are generally evaluated for parkerized finish. Look for finish consistency, sharp edges, and whether markings appear buffed or weakened.
Barrel Barrel markings, finish, wear, and proof marks should be photographed and compared to the production period. Barrel replacement is common on service pistols.
Small Parts Review hammer, safety, grip safety, trigger, slide stop, mainspring housing, grips, magazine, and pins. Parts may be original, period-correct replacements, arsenal replacements, or modern replacements.
Rebuild Marks Look for arsenal rebuild marks such as AA, SA, RIA, OG, ANAD, LEAD, or other depot marks. Rebuilds are legitimate military pistols, but they should be described accurately.
