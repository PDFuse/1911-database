Colt M1911 & M1911A1 Serial Number Ranges

Colt was the original manufacturer of the U.S. military M1911 and a major producer of the M1911A1. This page provides Colt serial number ranges and original collector guidance for identifying, researching, and documenting Colt military pistols.

WWI Colt M1911

Early Colt pistols should be evaluated by serial range, slide legend, frame markings, finish type, and period inspector initials.

WWII Colt M1911A1

WWII Colt pistols are commonly evaluated by inspection marks, parkerized finish, slide markings, barrel markings, and small parts.

Ithaca Duplicate Range

Serial numbers 856405 through 916404 require special review because Ithaca duplicated this range.

Important Colt / Ithaca Duplicate Serial Range

Duplicate Range Warning: Serial numbers from 856405 through 916404 were also used by Ithaca. A pistol in this range should not be identified by serial number alone. Confirm the actual maker by comparing frame markings, slide markings, inspector initials, finish, ordnance stamp, and other physical features.

Colt Collector Overview

Area What To Review
Serial Number Use the serial number to determine the likely production year and model period. The serial number is only the starting point and should not be used alone to authenticate a pistol.
Frame Markings Review United States Property marking, model marking, serial number font, inspector initials, proof marks, and ordnance acceptance stamp.
Slide Markings Compare the slide legend, patent/address lines, logo placement, and overall style to the serial range and production period.
Finish Early pistols may show original blue finishes, while later wartime pistols are generally evaluated for parkerized finish. Look for finish consistency, sharp edges, and whether markings appear buffed or weakened.
Barrel Barrel markings, finish, wear, and proof marks should be photographed and compared to the production period. Barrel replacement is common on service pistols.
Small Parts Review hammer, safety, grip safety, trigger, slide stop, mainspring housing, grips, magazine, and pins. Parts may be original, period-correct replacements, arsenal replacements, or modern replacements.
Rebuild Marks Look for arsenal rebuild marks such as AA, SA, RIA, OG, ANAD, LEAD, or other depot marks. Rebuilds are legitimate military pistols, but they should be described accurately.

Colt Verification Checklist

  • Confirm the serial number falls within the correct Colt production range.
  • If the serial is 856405 through 916404, compare Colt and Ithaca features before identifying the maker.
  • Compare slide markings to the production period.
  • Photograph inspector initials and ordnance stamp clearly.
  • Check whether the finish appears original, arsenal-applied, or commercially refinished.
  • Inspect barrel markings and determine whether the barrel appears period-correct or replaced.
  • Review small parts for correct style, finish, and wear consistency.
  • Look for arsenal rebuild marks before describing the pistol as original.
  • Use multiple sources and expert review before assigning authenticity or value.

Common Colt Red Flags

Red Flag Why It Matters
Weak or rounded markings May indicate buffing or commercial refinish.
Slide does not match serial period May indicate a replacement slide, arsenal rebuild, or later assembly.
Incorrect inspector initials Inspector marks should fit the manufacturer and production period.
Fresh finish over worn parts May indicate refinishing rather than original service wear.
Modern replacement parts Modern parts can affect collector classification and value.
Collector Note: Colt pistols were rebuilt, refinished, repaired, and mixed throughout military service life. A service-used or arsenal-rebuilt Colt can still be historically important, but it should not be described as untouched original unless the evidence supports that conclusion.
