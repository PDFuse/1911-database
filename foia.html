FOIA Research Guide for U.S. Military M1911 & M1911A1 Pistols

This page explains how Freedom of Information Act requests and archive research may help document U.S. military M1911 and M1911A1 pistols. It is written specifically for 1911 collectors researching serial numbers, CMP pistols, arsenal rebuild marks, depot history, contract records, and transfer records.

Important: FOIA is not a factory letter, appraisal, or authentication service. FOIA can only request existing government records. It usually cannot prove that a pistol is original, matching, valuable, or combat-used.

What FOIA Can Help With

Serial Number Research

Ask whether any existing records reference a specific M1911 or M1911A1 serial number, shipment block, storage group, transfer group, or disposal record.

Arsenal Rebuild Marks

Research AA, RIA, SA, OG, ANAD, LEAD, and other depot markings by requesting rebuild, inspection, overhaul, or stamping records.

CMP Provenance Context

FOIA may help with broader transfer and storage context, but CMP paperwork, data sheets, auction listings, and sales records remain the best individual-pistol provenance.

Contracts and Production

Research Colt, Remington Rand, Ithaca, Union Switch & Signal, Singer, barrels, magazines, spare parts, holsters, and procurement records.

What FOIA Usually Cannot Prove

QuestionReality
Was this exact pistol carried in combat?Usually no. Individual issue records are rarely available or searchable by serial number.
Is my pistol all original?No. Originality must be evaluated from the pistol: frame, slide, barrel, finish, marks, small parts, grips, and magazine.
Can the government appraise it?No. FOIA is not an appraisal process.
Can FOIA produce a complete serial-number history?Usually not. Most 1911 research requires combining serial ranges, physical evidence, CMP records, archive records, and collector references.

Sample FOIA Request for a Specific M1911/M1911A1

Subject: FOIA Request — U.S. Military M1911/M1911A1 Pistol Serial Number [INSERT SERIAL]

Pursuant to the Freedom of Information Act, I request copies of any existing records in your agency's possession that reference U.S. military M1911 or M1911A1 pistol serial number [INSERT SERIAL NUMBER].

If no records exist for that exact serial number, please search for records relating to the relevant serial-number production block, shipment block, storage group, transfer group, arsenal rebuild program, inspection program, or disposal/transfer process involving U.S. military M1911/M1911A1 pistols.

I am interested in inventory records, transfer records, depot rebuild records, inspection records, shipment records, property records, disposal records, policy memoranda, correspondence, or record schedules that may describe how these pistols were tracked or transferred. I request electronic copies where available.

Sample Request for Arsenal Rebuild Mark Research

Subject: Records Request — M1911/M1911A1 Arsenal Rebuild Mark [INSERT MARK]

I request copies of any existing records, manuals, inspection instructions, overhaul procedures, correspondence, or policy documents describing the rebuild, inspection, overhaul, refurbishment, or marking of U.S. military M1911 and M1911A1 pistols at [INSERT ARSENAL OR DEPOT].

I am particularly interested in records explaining the use of the marking [INSERT MARK, SUCH AS AA, RIA, SA, OG, ANAD, LEAD], including the time period used, inspection procedures, and whether the mark indicated rebuild, inspection, overhaul, or other processing.

How to Improve a Request

  • Use the exact serial number, manufacturer, model, caliber, and approximate year.
  • Ask for existing records, not opinions or authentication.
  • Include alternate search terms such as M1911, M1911A1, pistol, .45 automatic, Ordnance, depot, rebuild, and transfer.
  • Ask for the broader serial block if no exact serial-number record exists.
  • Request electronic records when available.
  • Save all responses, including no-records responses, as part of the research file.

How FOIA Fits Into Authentication

A strong 1911 research file usually combines several types of evidence. FOIA or archive responses should be read together with the pistol's physical evidence.

  • Serial-number range and year
  • Frame markings and inspector marks
  • Slide markings and proof marks
  • Barrel markings
  • Finish type and wear pattern
  • Small parts and grips
  • Magazine markings
  • Arsenal or depot rebuild marks
  • CMP paperwork, auction listing, museum record, or private provenance
  • FOIA or archive response
