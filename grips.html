M1911 & M1911A1 Grip Reference

Grips are one of the easiest features to compare when evaluating WWII M1911A1 pistols. Correct grips should match the production period, manufacturer, and overall service history.

Common Grip Types

Grip TypeCollector Notes
Double Diamond WalnutCommonly associated with earlier M1911 production.
Brown Plastic GripsFrequently encountered on WWII M1911A1 pistols.
Keyes Fibre GripsCommon wartime contractor grip style with molded reinforcing ribs, screw-hole rings, and mold numbers.
Replacement GripsMay indicate arsenal rebuild, repair, or post-service replacement.

Documented Example: WWII Keyes Fibre Grip Pair

This pair documents WWII Keyes Fibre brown plastic M1911A1 grips. The left panel is marked 5564063 and the right panel is marked 5564062. Observed features include brown phenolic plastic construction, molded reinforcing ribs, screw-hole reinforcement rings, and standard wartime M1911A1 checkering.

This documented grip pair is not tied to a specific pistol record unless provenance or pistol-mounted photographs are added. The style is generally period-correct for many WWII M1911A1 pistols and is commonly encountered on late-war Colt, Ithaca, Remington Rand, and Union Switch & Signal examples. Damage to one panel should be recorded as condition evidence.

Grip Evaluation Checklist

AreaWhat To Review
ColorCompare grip color to known wartime and service-era examples.
TextureReview checkering pattern and molding style.
InteriorLook for reinforcing ribs, screw-hole rings, mold numbers, and other molded details.
Mold NumbersDocument visible mold numbers such as 5564062 and 5564063.
WearWear should be consistent with the rest of the pistol.
FitPoor fit may indicate replacement or reproduction grips.
Period CorrectnessConfirm the grip style fits the serial range and manufacturer.
Collector Note: Grips were frequently replaced during military service. A replacement grip does not automatically reduce historical interest, but it should be documented accurately.
