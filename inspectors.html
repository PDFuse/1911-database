U.S. M1911 & M1911A1 Inspector Marks Reference

Inspector initials are one of the most important tools for identifying and verifying U.S. military M1911 and M1911A1 pistols. They should always be evaluated together with serial range, slide markings, frame markings, finish, proof marks, and rebuild marks.

Major Inspector Initials

Initials Inspector Approx. Period / Range Associated Manufacturers Collector Notes
J.M.G. Major John M. Gilbert Early M1911 production Colt Seen on very early Colt military M1911 pistols.
R.C.D. Major Robert C. Downie Early M1911 production and some WWII references Colt, Springfield Armory, Union Switch & Signal references Verify context carefully because R.C.D. appears in more than one reference setting.
W.G.P. Major Walter G. Penfield WWI-era M1911 production Colt, Springfield Armory Commonly associated with early U.S. military M1911 acceptance.
W.B. Colonel Waldemar Broberg Early WWII Colt M1911A1 production Colt Important for early WWII Colt verification before later G.H.D. inspection marks.
R.S. Colonel Robert Sears Pre-war / early WWII Colt M1911A1 production Colt Collector verification should compare serial range, finish, slide, and proof marks.
G.H.D. Colonel Guy H. Drewry WWII Colt production Colt Commonly associated with WWII Colt M1911A1 pistols.
F.J.A. Colonel Frank J. Atwood WWII contractor production Ithaca, Remington Rand, Union Switch & Signal Critical for identifying Ithaca pistols in the 856405 - 916404 duplicated serial range.
J.K.C. LTC John K. Clement / Christmas references Singer contract range Singer Associated with Singer contract pistols; verify carefully because Singer pistols are heavily counterfeited.
H.S. LTC Harrison Shaler Remington Rand references Remington Rand Use as a supporting identification reference with slide type and serial range.

Acceptance Marks vs. Rebuild Marks

Mark Type Meaning Collector Use
Inspector Initials Government acceptance inspection during original production. Helps verify manufacturer and production period.
Proof Marks Proof or firing inspection marks applied during inspection or acceptance. Should be checked with finish and wear patterns.
Arsenal Rebuild Marks Marks applied during repair, overhaul, or rebuild at an arsenal or depot. Can confirm service history, but changes originality classification.

Common Red Flags

  • Inspector mark inconsistent with the serial range or manufacturer.
  • Mark appears too sharp on a heavily worn or refinished pistol.
  • Mark appears washed out, buffed, or partially removed under refinish.
  • High-value makers such as Singer or US&S with unsupported or suspicious markings.
  • Duplicate serial range pistol identified without checking F.J.A., slide markings, and frame markings.
Collector Warning: Inspector marks should never be used alone to authenticate a pistol. Always compare the full pistol: serial range, frame, slide, finish, barrel, small parts, ordnance stamp, proof marks, and rebuild marks.
